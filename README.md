# Elevator_Tm412gc
Functional Requirements
Requirements 1–10 apply to every tier. Each tier adds behavior on top of the tier before it; Section 11 explains how the tiers are graded.

Clock, Timing, and Inputs (all tiers)

The system clock shall be 80 MHz, generated by the PLL, and configured before any other peripheral is initialized.
SysTick shall generate a periodic interrupt every 1 ms. All timing — travel between floors, the door timer, LED blinking, long-press detection, keypad scanning, and debouncing — shall be derived from this 1 ms tick.
The program shall not use software delay loops anywhere. The main loop must never block: while waiting for any timed event, the program must continue to monitor every input.
SW1, SW2, and every keypad key shall be debounced, driven by the SysTick tick. Document your sampling interval and the number of stable samples you require.
Only a new press (a released-to-pressed transition) counts. Holding a key or switch down shall register as exactly one press; contact bounce shall never register as a second press.
Modular Software Design (all tiers)

Modular structure: the program shall be divided into modules (for example: timing, keypad, hall buttons, indicator outputs, request queue, and the elevator controller). Each module has a .c file and a matching .h file that declares only its public interface. main() only initializes the modules and runs the main loop, and the call graph shall be hierarchical (no module calls back up into a higher-level module).
Functional abstraction: each task shall be performed by a well-named function with a single purpose (for example, showing a floor, adding a request, or reading a key). The same operation shall not be repeated as copied code in several places; write it once as a function and call it.
Complexity abstraction: the design shall be layered. Low-level driver modules hide all register access behind simple functions; the elevator controller uses only those interfaces and never reads or writes GPIO, SysTick, or other peripheral registers directly. The details of the request queue and the state machine are hidden behind their own interfaces.
Portability: all hardware-specific details (register addresses, port and pin choices, bit masks, and timing constants such as 2 s travel and 5 s door time) shall be confined to driver modules and named constants. Moving an LED or the relay to a different pin shall require changes in one driver module only. No unexplained numeric constants ("magic numbers") in the controller logic.
Security: data and helper functions used only inside a module shall be declared static so other modules cannot reach them; other modules access a module's data only through its public functions. Variables shared between the SysTick ISR and the main loop shall be declared volatile and documented. Public functions shall validate their inputs (for example, a request for floor 0 or 7 is rejected, and a full queue never writes past the end of its array).
Tier 1 — Basic Elevator (one request at a time)

After power-up or reset, the car shall be at floor 1 with the door closed: the floor 1 position LED on (solid), the RGB indicator showing the floor 1 color, and both direction LEDs off.
Exactly one position LED shall be on at any time, showing the car's current floor. It is solid while the door is closed and blinks at 2 Hz while the door is open.
SW1 shall call the car to floor 1 and SW2 shall call it to floor 5. Keypad keys 1–5 shall request the corresponding floor.
The car shall travel one floor every 2 seconds. At each step, the current floor's LED turns off and the next floor's LED turns on, and the RGB indicator changes to that floor's color (Section 6).
Direction indicator: while the car is traveling up, only the UP LED shall be on; while it is traveling down, only the DOWN LED shall be on; whenever the car is not moving (idle, door open, emergency stop, or stopped in maintenance), both shall be off.
The direction indicator shall be built from exactly one single-channel relay module and two LEDs. The current for each direction LED shall pass through the relay contacts; an LED wired straight from a GPIO pin to ground (or to 3.3 V) without going through the relay does not meet this requirement. No second relay, transistor-only LED driver, or other switching device may replace the relay.
The wrong direction LED shall never light, even for a moment, when the car starts, stops, or reverses direction. This must be achieved without software delay loops (Requirement 3).
When the car arrives at a requested floor, the door shall open automatically. The door closes after 5 seconds, or immediately when # is pressed. Pressing * while the door is open restarts the 5-second timer.
When the door closes and there is no pending request, the car shall stay at that floor with the door closed (idle). While idle, the keypad remains active: * reopens the door and a floor key requests a trip.
A request (hall call or keypad) for the floor the car is already on shall open the door, or restart the timer if it is already open, without any travel.
Safety: while the car is moving, the door keys (* and #) shall have no effect.
In Tier 1 the car serves one request at a time: any request made while the car is already serving another request is ignored.
Tier 2 — Request Queue

Requests from SW1, SW2, and the keypad shall be stored in a queue that holds at least 8 requests, replacing the one-at-a-time behavior of Tier 1. Hall calls shall be accepted and stored even while the car is moving.
A floor may be requested again while it is already in the queue (for example 1, 2, 1, 2 queues four trips). Only an immediate repeat — the same floor as the newest request, such as pressing the same key twice in a row — shall be ignored.
Requests shall be served in the order they were received (first-in, first-out). When the door closes, the car heads to the oldest pending request.
Floor keys 1–5 shall be accepted and queued in every state of normal operation, including while the car is moving, so passengers can enter several floors in a row. A floor key pressed while the door is open also restarts the 5-second door timer.
If SW1 and SW2 are both pressed, the press recognized first is queued first. If both are recognized in the same 1 ms tick, apply a tie-break rule of your choice and document it.
Tier 3 — Stops on the Way and Emergency Stop

The oldest pending request sets the direction of travel. If the car reaches a floor that is also in the queue while traveling, it shall stop there, remove that floor from the queue, and open the door, before continuing.
Emergency stop: key 0 shall be accepted in every state except maintenance, including while the car is moving (the only key that works during travel). The car stops at the floor it is on, both direction LEDs turn off, the position LED is solid (door closed), and the RGB indicator blinks magenta at 1 Hz.
During an emergency stop, * and # keep their normal meaning: * opens the door (position LED blinks) and # closes it. The door does not close automatically during a stop. Floor keys have no effect. Hall calls are still accepted into the queue.
Key 9 releases the emergency stop. Pending requests are kept, and normal operation resumes (if the door is open, the normal 5-second door timer starts).
Tier 4 — Maintenance Mode

Holding SW1 or SW2 for 5 seconds shall put the elevator in maintenance from any state, including an emergency stop. The initial press is treated as a normal hall call; when the 5-second hold is reached, that hall call is cancelled.
On entering maintenance, all pending requests are cleared and the RGB indicator blinks cyan at 1 Hz. The car travels (2 seconds per floor, with the direction indicator showing UP or DOWN) to the floor of the switch that was held — floor 1 for SW1, floor 5 for SW2 — reversing direction if necessary, then opens the door and keeps it open (position LED blinking).
In maintenance, every input is ignored except a press of the same switch that started maintenance, which closes the door and returns the car to normal idle operation at that floor. The other switch has no effect.
Keypad keys 6, 7, and 8 shall have no effect in any state (unless used by your bonus feature).
